Open-weight models are months behind the closed frontier.

The gap is how long the best open-weight AI model takes to match the most capable closed models on the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI), which combines many benchmarks into one score. The latest figures are loading. If they do not appear, turn on JavaScript or read data.json.

How the gap is measured

The method is Epoch AI's. Each day, it takes the open-weight model with the highest score on the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) released so far and finds the newest record-setting closed model that it has plausibly caught up to. The gap is the time since that closed model's release.

An open-weight model has caught up when it scores higher than the closed model in at least 5% of 500 paired bootstrap resamples of the benchmark results, which means the closed model is not significantly better. The headline is the daily gap averaged over the 12 months to the latest record-setting release. Over the same period, the best closed model led by an average of ECI points.

Today's build could not rerun the bootstrap, so matches use a normal approximation from the published intervals, which is slightly more permissive.

What it leaves out

The gap may understate how far open-weight models trail. Epoch AI notes that open-weight models tend to do worse against closed models on private benchmarks than on public ones, and that AI companies do not always release their most capable models.

A matched score also says nothing about what a model costs to run, or about whether it reached that score by training on a closed model's outputs.

What it leaves out

The trendline figure smooths over single releases and is read at the latest Chinese record, so it does not grow while no new Chinese record appears. It assumes steady progress on both sides, so it is slow to show a change of pace.

Models from developers outside China and the U.S. are left out. Only released models with enough public benchmark results get an ECI score, and AI companies do not always release their most capable models.
